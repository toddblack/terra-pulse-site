# Strata Pulse for Android — Privacy Policy

*Last updated: 14 November 2026*

Strata Pulse for Android is an app for exploring earthquakes alongside space
weather and the Earth's magnetic field, on a 3D globe. It is built by Todd
Black. This policy covers the Android app.

## The short version

- **No account, no sign-in, no tracking.** The app has no analytics, no
  telemetry, no advertising and no crash reporting. Nothing about you or how
  you use the app is sent to the developer.
- **It never asks for your location**, contacts, camera, microphone or files.
  The only permissions it uses are internet access (to fetch public science
  data) and vibration (for its in-app earthquake alerts).
- **It does fetch public science data from the internet.** Like any request
  your phone makes, those reach the servers listed below, which see your IP
  address.

## What the app stores

Nothing of yours. The app keeps no settings, history or location. Your layer
choices last only while the app is open. Android's web view may cache
downloaded map tiles and data files on the phone, as a browser does;
clearing the app's storage or uninstalling it removes them.

Earthquake alerts are worked out on the phone, from the same public
earthquake feed the globe shows, and only while the app is open.

## What the app sends over the internet

Strata Pulse downloads public scientific data directly from the organisations
that publish it. Each request goes straight from your phone to that
organisation's server, never through a server run by the developer. As with
any web request, that server can see your **IP address** and the app's name
and version. Their own privacy policies govern what they do with it.

- **Earthquakes:** the U.S. Geological Survey (earthquake.usgs.gov).
- **Space weather** (aurora, ionosphere, solar wind, the Dst index): NOAA's
  Space Weather Prediction Center (services.swpc.noaa.gov).
- **The Kp index:** GFZ Helmholtz Centre for Geosciences (kp.gfz.de).
- **The maps:** OpenFreeMap (tiles.openfreemap.org), which serves OpenStreetMap
  data, for the Basic map; and NOAA's National Centers for Environmental
  Information (gis.ngdc.noaa.gov) for the Relief map. Map requests name the
  part of the globe on screen, which is not your phone's location.
- **Live seismometers**, only while that panel is open: the seismic data
  centres EarthScope (rtserve.earthscope.org, service.earthscope.org), GeoNet
  New Zealand (link.geonet.org.nz, service.geonet.org.nz), GEOFON at GFZ
  (geofon.gfz.de) and IRIS's station catalogue (service.iris.edu). To find
  stations, the app sends them the spot you picked on the globe. That is the
  point you pressed, not your phone's location, which the app never reads.
- **Volcanoes**, only while a volcano layer is switched on: the Smithsonian
  Institution's Global Volcanism Program (webservices.volcano.si.edu) for the
  list of volcanoes, NOAA's Aviation Weather Center (aviationweather.gov) for
  volcanic ash advisories, and the U.S. Geological Survey (volcanoes.usgs.gov)
  for volcano alert levels.
- **Volcano cameras**, only while a volcano's details are open: the U.S.
  Geological Survey (volcview.wr.usgs.gov), the Japan Meteorological Agency
  (www.data.jma.go.jp) and GeoNet New Zealand (images.geonet.org.nz). The
  pictures load straight from those servers.
- **Pictures from NASA-ISRO's NISAR satellite**, only when you open the
  "From space" card on an earthquake or a volcano: NASA's Earthdata search
  (cmr.earthdata.nasa.gov) to find the satellite's passes, NASA's Global
  Imagery Browse Services (gibs.earthdata.nasa.gov) for radar pictures, and
  the Alaska Satellite Facility (nisar.asf.earthdatacloud.nasa.gov), whose
  pictures are delivered through Amazon CloudFront
  (d1mv8zhcvry6x4.cloudfront.net). These requests carry the location of the
  earthquake or volcano you opened, not your phone's location.

Links you tap (a quake's USGS page, the maps' credits to OpenFreeMap,
OpenMapTiles, OpenStreetMap and NOAA, Cesium's site, a volcano's page at the
Smithsonian, USGS volcano notices, and observatories' own camera pages,
including USGS's YouTube channel) open in your browser, under that site's own
policy.

Google Play, which installs and updates the app, handles data under
Google's own privacy policy.

## Children

Strata Pulse is a general-audience science app. It does not knowingly collect
any information from anyone, children included.
